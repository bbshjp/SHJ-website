Permits

The dreaded word that no one wants to hear, right? Permits, they equal paperwork, plan review, downtime, added fees, and more. For some projects, that's true, permits do require paperwork, plan reviews, downtime, added fees, and a lot more, but most of the time this is not the case. Most stand alone permits can be pulled the same day as the job is sold and/or started and most inspections can be scheduled for the day after the portion of the job to be inspected was completed.

            Most of my conversations with people who do not want to pull permits always have the same signature. The superior intellect and knowledge of thier own home and how no one should be aloud to get in their way and tell them what to do. Let's be honest though, it's not your home. Even if you own the home outright at the time, it still will belong to someone else done the road, but forget about that for now. Let's discuss the need for permits.

            To begin with, it's for your protection. That's right, even if you do know better then any trade professonal, engineer, or achitech the permit still protects you. Consider it as a backup to ensure that you haven't missed something; an extra set of eyes as it were. In addition to that, these inspectors have one job; to know the building code. Imagine for a moment that a mobile home park had a bunch of new homes installed without tie downs. As your home flys down the road during a storm, who's responsible? The builder? The contrator who installed the home? The park itself? The new homeowner? What if your home was tied down and your neighbors wasn't? What if your neighbor and you both agreed that permits are just taxes and neither one of you pulled one. Will your neighbor appear differnet to you if his or her home damgaes your home or worse yet harms you?

            Permits help to allivate those concerns and to build on more unified standards. They also help to ensure a piece of mind mentality to the individual homeowner who may not be as knowledgable as you are. In today's world, it is far safer for a builder, contractor, and/or homeowner to put a permit and ensure that building codes are met then to take the chance and not pull a permit which potentially comes with fines, liability issues, and regret. Always error on the side of caution in this case.
